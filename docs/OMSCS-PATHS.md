## Choose subjects

1. The next-term selector is the first planning action. Open **Prior courses &
   return** when you need to adjust the basis. A fresh device now starts from
   the approved public academic history. Saved empty/custom profiles remain
   authoritative; you can also add previously completed subjects to the temporary draft;
   leave unknown grades unrecorded. This does not save academic records. A
   reported passing/unknown-grade subject moves out of new free choices into
   prior work instead of counting twice; D/F attempts do not reduce the budget.
   If return approval is already confirmed, mark that in the draft: the gap
   notice becomes historical context, not an instruction to apply again.
2. Choose a **career direction**, then fill the five specialization slots:
   one algorithms/design, two AI-core and two specialization electives.
   Eligible prior subjects can fill slots. Five slots does not mean five new
   enrollments. Optional worked examples replace draft choices explicitly.
3. Fill the additional free-elective budget. Remove an unwanted example
   before selecting its replacement. Search by course/area or filter by career
   fit; clearing filters restores every catalogue option.
4. Open **My shortlist**. Review the new subjects separately from reported
   prior work. Note the list or expand its plain text, use **Select shortlist
   text**, then the phone/browser copy command. No clipboard upload or sharing
   service is called. Incomplete/conflicting choices stay clearly labelled.
   **First-term subject** lets you select one starting subject while later terms
   remain proposals. Only currently selected new subjects are offered. The
   preparation-order suggestion is not a final choice or a booked seat.

Course benefits, tradeoffs, tools and roadmap links are behind each subject's
question-mark button, available by touch, keyboard and hover. Redundant
introductory prose is deleted rather than relocated wholesale. The optional
**Course choices & contextual help** tutorial teaches the real controls.

The full 830/216-combination browser and calendar are collapsed initially.
The semester proposal is an optional check after subject selection,
not a reservation. An explicit starting subject is ordered first without
changing the subject set; readiness warnings still apply. If that course
becomes prior work or leaves the draft, the calendar stops until the learner
deliberately resets or replaces it rather than silently proposing a retake.
Draft choices last only while this page stays open:
refreshing or leaving resets them. Internal roadmap links open separately
so inspecting a connection does not intentionally discard this draft.

## What a subject can contribute

Every current catalogue subject has an authored benefit, opportunity cost and
explicit links to existing CareerOS roadmaps. **Career direction** compares AI
engineering, AI-cloud/solutions architecture and forward deployed engineering.
**Direct support**, **Supporting depth** and **Specialist detour** are qualitative
judgments, not numeric scores, university endorsements or employment forecasts.
Changing this emphasis changes explanations/filters, never subject selections.

Connections use the actual mission identities: Pattern/Algorithm Forge for
algorithmic foundations; System Forge/Fabric Core for resource/distributed
reasoning; Blueprint for architecture; Neural Edge for AI/agent-cloud systems;
Fieldcraft and Side Income for delivery, discovery and customer value. A
connection is not automatic learning credit or proof that a course teaches
Azure, Service Fabric or production agent operations.

Some important choices are deliberately not flattened into equivalent labels:
GA versus SDP is proof/algorithm depth versus software delivery; DC versus
AOS/SDCC is distributed protocols versus broader/deeper cloud-system work;
GPU and HPC emphasize different computation/performance layers; NLP versus CV
depends on the data/application domain. HCI strengthens discovery/adoption
without substituting for model or systems implementation. RL is not compulsory
merely because an application is called an agent.

## What is counted

OMSCS requires **10 courses / 30 credits**, not ten credits. AI uses five
courses / 15 credits: one algorithms/design course, two AI-core courses and two
specialization electives. The remaining five courses / 15 credits are free
electives. Each subject is counted once.

Reported completed courses reduce a conditional planning budget. Unknown grades
are disclosed, not treated as verified credit. Specialization roles require B
or better, free electives C or better, and the actual cumulative GPA must be at
least 3.0. D/F attempts do not reduce the passing-credit budget; an entered C
cannot silently satisfy a B-minimum specialization slot.

The specifically approved academic starter is intentionally public app data.
Other private planning history remains outside repository source, issues,
shared continuity and production output. Academic self-reports are not a
verified transcript or learning completion.

## Complete menus, not one prescribed route

The **current OMSCS AI page** offers two online algorithms/design choices, six
online AI-core choices and six explicit online electives. It also permits unused
core courses as electives, including the other algorithms/design choice.
The complete online choice system has **1,650 role allocations across 830
distinct five-course sets**. These are different dimensions: the same subjects
can sometimes fill the slots in more than one way.

The **conservative catalog comparison** uses AI/KBAI/ML for the two AI-core
slots and nine electives explicitly shared with the older general MSCS table.
It does not assume the newer excess-core exception for that older table. This
lens has **216 distinct allocations/subject sets**. It is an intentionally
conservative comparison, not a declaration that the student's official record
must use one rule set or that an exception has been approved.

Campus-only and older-table-only reference lists are removed from the normal
UI, not offered as alternative online subjects. Historical research retains
its excluded-source evidence. Fresh machine comparison matches all 78 regular
course identities, URLs and foundational flags, plus all 14 bold online AI
menu entries. Catalogue membership still does not guarantee a future offering.

Completed compatible courses can be reused in different legal roles. Filtering
for maximum reuse finds efficient plans; other allocations can require more new
specialization subjects and leave fewer new free-elective choices. The ten-course
total remains the target. An explicit grade conflict stays visible.

## Free electives are genuine choices

The reviewed public catalogue has **78 regular-course entries**, including
13 whose subject code is neither CS nor CSE. All are browsable/searchable;
course-topic identities remain distinct rather than collapsing CS 8803 topics.
The planner enforces the maximum six non-CS/CSE credits, represented as two
three-credit courses. Seminars are one-credit pass/fail and do not count.

The selected free subjects are replaceable editorial suggestions. Clear them,
choose another capability-oriented example, or build a custom set. Existing
selections are never silently discarded when a specialization change creates
a duplicate or overfilled budget; the conflict is shown with removal controls.

Free-choice counts describe the compatible completions of the *currently
selected* allocation. They are not summed across specialization sets as a
misleading global count of unique complete degrees, and millions of complete
plans are not materialized into the DOM. Pagination covers the entire filtered
specialization/course catalogue, not a hidden truncated subset.

Capability areas and example bundles are authored navigation. All 78 official
public overviews were read for the benefit/tradeoff/roadmap guidance. The ten
original companion guides and ten additional readiness/tool overviews retain
their separate deeper review scope. Other subjects explicitly disclose that
complete content, readiness, tools and assessed workload were not reviewed.
A short public overview is not a complete future syllabus.

## Semester and return checks

The calendar uses only selected new subjects. One regular-term course is the
default; later summers and a two-course heavy regular-term scenario are
explicit controls. Summer stays one course. Required course ordering and
recommended preparation are separate:

- **SDCC / CS 6211:** the linked faculty syllabus states prior OS study or
  instructor consent; the current OMSCS page expects AOS with A or high B, or
  equivalent study elsewhere. A missing prerequisite, unknown grade or plain B
  does not establish eligibility. The calendar stops until a qualifying prior
  report, planned earlier AOS or explicit allowed-alternative confirmation
  resolves it. Planned AOS and SDCC never share a term, even at heavy load;
  future qualifying grades stay conditional. Weekly 90-minute live meetings
  remain visible. The linked sample is dated, not a future section contract.
- **DL / CS 7643:** formal introductory ML is expected and self-study does not
  count according to the public page. Equivalent formal study can satisfy that
  background; no registrar-enforced CS 7641-only rule is invented. Earlier
  planned ML is conditional, not already completed study. An unsupported
  first-DL choice retains a visible background warning.
- **Database Implementation / CS 6422:** required foundation-level algorithms
  and systems coursework is distinguished from recommended OS/database
  familiarity and C++/systems readiness. No mandatory GIOS/CS 6400 chain is
  fabricated.
- **Big Data for Health / CSE 6250:** the OMSCS overview lists prior statistics
  grades/data subjects, while the linked Fall 2026 faculty page places those
  formal conditions under an undergraduate cross-listing and permits learned
  equivalent background for its graduate cross-listing. The UI flags the
  graduate-section rule for confirmation instead of imposing campus-only
  undergraduate degree choices.

AOS accepts relevant OS/industry background; DC explicitly states no enforced
prerequisites; GPU lists preparation courses as beneficial. These do not become
mandatory named-course locks. **Entry confirmations** are tab-local,
self-reported checks with no grade rewrite, course credit or university check.
Only documented cases are modeled; current sections may add requirements.

The Registrar's current return policy triggers a readmission check after
three or more consecutive absent terms, **including summer**. Approval is not
guaranteed and the explorer does not know whether it has already been granted.
The general Spring deadline is December 1 in the preceding year; other
standings have earlier deadlines. Confirm the current applicable conditions.

The same policy says readmission uses the catalog current at return; retaining
an older one requires a separate approved request. This matters alongside the
disagreement between the OMSCS specialization page and general catalog.

The program requires two foundational B-or-better courses in the first
12 months. Foundational markers are preserved, but unknown grades do not verify
that condition. Its FAQ describes credits as valid for six years from the
semester earned. Breaks and readmission do not automatically renew old credits.
Six-year warnings are watchpoints for official confirmation, not fabricated
graduation deadlines or extensions. Incomplete subject choices are never
labelled a complete graduation estimate.

## Implementation and privacy boundary

`src/domain/omscs/paths` owns the static public catalogue, explicit rule lenses,
complete role/set enumeration and pure budget/calendar functions.
`src/omscs/paths` loads separately and keeps draft choices in component memory.
`careerFit.ts` holds explicit editorial associations, not keyword matching or
an AI adviser. The pure shortlist retains role/conflict information; its UI
separates proposed new subjects from reported prior subjects. The draft history
editor validates the same planning schema and never calls workspace persistence.
Its optional confirmed-return flag and explicit first-term choice are likewise
tab-local, not new saved academic fields or official verification.
`requirements.ts` models source-backed entry distinctions and the hard
earlier-term edge. `CourseEntryInfo` exposes compact status and linked detail
on choices, shortlists and calendars. Required-background confirmations remain
separate from academic records and do not alter the degree-credit calculation.
Draft choices add no workspace schema, journal cell or academic save. The
separate starter initializer uses the existing atomic `omscsPlan` setting and
a device-only initialization-version preference, not another progress ledger.
The original mission's 24 checkpoints and source/recall/Atlas rules are unchanged.

The earlier external private-preview process and input remain outside the
repository. Both current development and production planners ignore that old
document override and derive their initial basis from saved academic records.
No actual owner browser profile is automated, and drafts grant no progress or XP.
